Peer coaching · Mentoring · Advisory

Mental Wealth.

Transforming perceived mental illness into a blessing.

Begin a conversation ↗

That possibility is personal to me. Experiences once interpreted as evidence of my limitations became the starting point for my education, relationships, and life’s work. It took years of inquiry and support to understand them differently.

Mental Wealth is where I bring that experience to others. We can explore distress and uncertainty alongside creativity, spirituality, desire, and purpose. There is room for what hurts and for what you hope your life might become.

01One to one

Coaching and mentoring

Individual conversations about what you’re living through and how you want to respond. My interests include neurodiversity, perceived mental illness, mental health, and spiritual, existential, moral, and religious emergencies.

02With families

Understanding and choices

Help making sense of unfamiliar experiences, talking across differences, and considering options for support. We attend to relationships and to the wishes and agency of the person at the center.

03With practitioners and organizations

Education and collaboration

Work with therapists, psychiatrists, hospitals, holistic practitioners, nonprofits, and companies. I contribute a peer perspective to staff learning, service design, and conversations about alternatives to existing care.

Private arrangements · By conversation

Private & Concierge Mentoring

I work with ultra-high-net-worth individuals and families in trusted, private settings. Depending on the circumstances, this can include extended engagement and live-in concierge peer support, bringing our work into the routines, relationships, and decisions of daily life.

I want to put my cards on the table about why I offer this work. Wealth does not remove the need for understanding, companionship, or help. These clients deserve the same care and respect as anyone else. I also intend to generate substantial income to realize my larger dream: mental-health reformation across the country, with new infrastructure for care, community, and meaningful lives.

Everyone deserves help. Building a financially ambitious practice is one way I intend to resource that wider commitment.

In development

Spiritual Psychiatry

I am developing an adjunct peer-support offering alongside a board-certified adult, child and adolescent, and addiction psychiatrist with decades of clinical experience and a professorship at a major university medical school. His work explores spiritual and religious experience in relation to addiction, trauma, and mental health.

The intention is to bring clinical expertise and lived experience into conversation around spiritual emergence, distress, meaning, and integration. My contribution is peer mentoring and education, alongside the psychiatrist’s separate clinical role. The collaboration and arrangements are being developed.

Retreats & Group Experiences

I am also exploring retreats and group events at the Center for Spiritual Awakening in Holmdel, New Jersey, sponsored by the Human Faith Project nonprofit. These would offer opportunities for shared inquiry into spirituality, dignity, relationships, and creativity, welcoming people of any faith or none.

Program details and dates will be shared as arrangements are confirmed.

Understanding the mental health system and its implications

I have been irrevocably harmed by psychiatry, the mental health system, medications, and the principles and values used to justify my treatment. I know many others who have paid a lasting price, and people around the world have spoken about similar harm. Having someone with that experience in your corner can matter. I am not categorically against psychiatry, services, or medication. I am honest, brutally honest, about how dangerous, consuming, and victimizing they can be. You deserve to understand those possibilities before deciding what care to accept. I’ve paid the price, and I want to help others make informed choices so they don’t have to.

Empowerment, freedom, respect, and agency.

People deserve to understand what they are entering when they engage psychiatric services. I help individuals and families examine programs, medications, and approaches, including their implications, potential consequences, and the questions worth asking.

I know how much personal attention these experiences deserve, and how difficult an overstretched system can make it to receive that care. My work can complement traditional services or help people explore alternatives and assemble a different network of support.

Our conversations may draw on narrative practices, archetypal systems, holistic approaches, or AI-assisted reflection. We discuss what each can offer and use it where it helps your inquiry.

Law enforcement & crisis response

The person.
The encounter.
What happens next.

Lived-experience education and dialogue for police departments, officers, and crisis-response partners.

Explore a conversation or session ↗

The person inside the encounter

I speak from experience of losing my freedom and being taken into psychiatric care. I bring that perspective into conversations with officers about what it feels like to be the person in distress, how an encounter unfolds, and what dignity and agency mean in those moments.

My focus includes the ride, the handoff, and the care that follows. Connecting someone to a service is consequential. Where are they being taken? What might happen there? What other support or community-based alternatives could be available?

Did you know?

Part of this work is an introduction to ideas, resources, and literature officers may not have encountered: the Z65.8 category for moral, religious, or spiritual problems, peer respite homes, EmPATH crisis-stabilization services, interfaith approaches, and writing on spiritual emergence and spiritual emergency.

I bring my peer story into that exploration, connecting personal experience with questions officers can carry into their work. What else might help us understand this person? What support exists beyond the familiar options? Who in the community could help? Sessions bring awareness, education, lived experience, and open dialogue together.

The care that follows

Through talks, facilitated discussions, and advisory collaboration, I help connect lived experience with the practical questions facing law enforcement. This work can complement existing crisis-intervention education and peer perspectives, while opening conversation about respite, relationships, and better pathways beyond hospitalization.

Officers do not control the whole system, and what happens after a handoff is not theirs alone to carry. But wouldn’t it feel good to understand the wider picture and know that the person you’ve helped is being welcomed somewhere they can feel safe, be heard, and receive thoughtful care?

That is part of the invitation: to explore what peer respite houses and other community-based alternatives can offer, where available and appropriate, and to help create more of those possibilities. I want officers to feel connected to the people and places receiving someone, and to take satisfaction in contributing to a more humane path through distress.

A life inside and beyond the system

I speak about my life inside and beyond the psychiatric system: what happened, what I’ve seen, and the hope I’ve carried out of experiences that once seemed to close off my future.

That story includes the harsh realities of mental-health services and of living at the edges. I talk about being misunderstood, losing freedom, questioning the explanations I was given, and finding people and possibilities that helped me build a life.

When wounds become gifts

Over time, I came to recognize my wounds as gifts. The experiences that hurt me also shaped my attention, my questions, and my capacity to meet others. Much of what I bring to my community now grew from that recognition.

Education & genius

There’s a genius
born every minute.

When I speak of genius, I’m referring to the word’s original sense: the inherent essence and spirit we’re born with, which only we can offer to the world. That genius may take many forms over a lifetime. It may become an exceptional skill or craft. Ultimately, I mean the exceptional human being you are, living your destiny.

My understanding of education holds two related ideas: nurturing and cultivating, and drawing out what is already within someone. I want to help people meet their genius and give it conditions in which to grow.

This is why passions matter in my work. What someone loves can tell us something that an assessment or a diagnosis misses. Taking it seriously can change the conversation about who they are and what they might do.

In love

In kinship

Read my extended biography ↗

From the inside

At nineteen, while studying film in New York, I was diagnosed with bipolar disorder and involuntarily hospitalized. Over the years that followed, I experienced the psychiatric system from inside it. Questions about dignity, freedom, and who gets to interpret another person’s experience became unavoidable.

I later completed UMDNJ’s two-year psychosocial rehabilitation certificate program. During my internship at CSPNJ in Eatontown, I developed The Happiness Agenda, working with people through their interests and passions.

An education in the world

For the past fifteen years, my education has continued through grassroots mental-health inquiry and coaching, international travel, and collaborative communities. I worked directly with the late futurist Barbara Marx Hubbard and co-created Synergy Hub 1.0 in Rotterdam, a sixteen-month experiment in gift economy, shared resources, and self-organization.

I’m an autodidact. Approximately 180 college credits, twenty-five years of independent study, and the experience of putting ideas into practice have shaped how I think. I do not hold a college degree. I bring the knowledge I earned through that study, my formal certificate training, and a life lived in inquiry.

A community around the work

I’m part of an international community of change agents, practitioners, researchers, artists, and experts across many fields. These are people I learn from, turn to, and collaborate with. In mental health, those relationships include journalists, clinicians, interfaith leaders, peer advocates, and technologists actively working to change how people are understood and supported.

Today I’m bringing those experiences into education, peer mentoring, film, and technology. The same questions run through the work: how do we recognize what each person brings, care for one another, and organize ourselves so more becomes possible?

Principles and values

What animates
my work.

I call my larger practice evolutionary and spiritual philanthropy. Philanthropy means love of humanity to me. It begins in how I meet people and extends into what we make together, how resources move, and whose lives benefit.

In What I Actually Sacrificed, I wrote about giving up control and dominance while still wanting to lead. That distinction matters in mentoring and collaboration: I can bring conviction, initiative, and experience while respecting another person’s freedom.

Money, creativity, and entrepreneurship have a place in that practice. I want the work to sustain the people doing it and help build the communities, opportunities, and forms of care we need.

The voices below have accompanied that inquiry. They speak to coming alive, remaining humble, expressing our gifts, and taking responsibility for what we can contribute.

At the edge of possibility

“Any useful idea about the future should appear ridiculous.”
- Jim Dator

Futurist Joseph Voros calls the dividing line between futures we consider possible and those we dismiss as preposterous the Clarke-Dator Boundary. Crossing it means entertaining ideas our present assumptions might reject as ridiculous or impossible. Those judgments can change: the Moon landing once belonged in that territory.

For me, stepping into the preposterous means staying curious beyond that boundary, long enough to discover what we might bring into being.

Evolutionary & Spiritual Philanthropy

The love of humanity.
Put into practice.

For private philanthropists, family offices, and people and projects reinventing how we give, receive, and care for the planet as a whole.

Explore your next bold step ↗

When I say philanthropy, I mean the love of humanity. My inquiry begins with who we are being as we give: our relationship to money, power, trust, and one another. The way resources are offered and received is already shaping the world they are meant to serve.

I work with people who want to bring that inquiry into their lives and philanthropic work. You may be reconsidering your giving, developing an initiative, or seeking a bold new way to put your resources in service of life. We explore what you care about, what your resources could make possible, and how to support people’s agency without making generosity a means of control.

Through education, dialogue, advisory collaboration, and introductions where there is a fit, I help connect inner commitments with practical choices. That can include clarifying an intention, developing a project, exploring gift-based and regenerative approaches, or bringing people and disciplines into a shared effort.

Money matters, and so do time, relationships, land, creativity, and the freedom to act. I’m interested in how these capacities can work together for the greatest benefit of humanity and the living planet. I bring thirty years of inquiry and lived experimentation, and an invitation to ask better questions together.

Begin where you intend to arrive.

If our destination is a world shaped by generosity, dignity, and cooperation, those qualities belong in the beginning: in the relationships we build, the decisions we share, and the way resources move.

For Philanthropists & Investors

Let’s be honest.
My ambitions are preposterous.

My ambitions are preposterous, and I’m putting the practical invitation on the table. I want to help catalyze mental health reformation across this country and beyond, bringing regenerative film, community, and new infrastructure together at a scale that can change people’s lives. I’m not going to pretend that’s a modest undertaking.

I welcome philanthropists, investors, and advisors who feel a deep resonance with these missions and my portfolio of innovations and thought leadership. You may be drawn to one project, to the connections across them, or to supporting me as the person bringing this work into being.

A five-year runway

$95,000

Per year · Personal gifts

$95,000 a year for five years, offered as unconditional personal gifts. This is a runway for me to thrive as the source, founder, visionary, and leader: time and capacity to create, develop, connect, and carry the work. One person could offer the full amount, or many people could share in it. These are gifts, freely given, without purchasing ownership, control, or obligations in return.

$1 million

Annual operating budget

A separate annual operating budget of $1 million. This would begin the rollout of the mental health reformation and its Evolutionary and Spiritual Philanthropy Fund, resourcing the people, development, production, partnerships, and coordination that turn the vision into work on the ground. The five-year horizon gives that work room to take root. Larger film and infrastructure ambitions would have their own budgets and pathways.

Begin as we mean to become.

I’m committed to generating revenue and funding this work myself wherever I can, acting as my own philanthropist and investor. I also welcome others who want to nourish its beginnings.

For me, unconditional gifting must precede or accompany outside investment. The gift stands on its own, without purchasing ownership, control, or a promise of return. Any investment is a separate agreement.

That order matters. My work has a destination rooted in evolutionary and spiritual philanthropy. Its beginnings must embody that same generosity, freedom, and trust. How we support the person, how resources move, and how we enter relationships are already part of what we’re creating. I want to begin where I intend to arrive.

Perhaps we’re looking for each other.

I’m excited by the possibility of a circle of advisors, philanthropists, and investors bringing their different capacities to this work. I would also welcome one extraordinary ally able to contribute substantially, or a combination of both.

I don’t know how long it will take me to generate the resources the larger vision deserves. If you have substantial financial capacity and share these ambitions, we could catalyze a quick, decisive, large-scale effort: the practical moonshot of regenerative film and lasting change in mental health infrastructure.

You may be looking for somewhere to put your resources that feels equal to your own ambitions for the world. I’m looking for people with whom these possibilities can become real. That invitation is always open.
